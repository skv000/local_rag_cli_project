# NASA Artemis Program Overview

## Mission Goals
The Artemis campaign is a NASA-led international human spaceflight program. Its primary goal is to return humans to the Moon, specifically targeting the lunar South Pole region, and to establish a sustainable long-term human presence there by the late 2020s. The program also serves as a critical stepping stone toward future human missions to Mars.

## Key Hardware Components
1. **Space Launch System (SLS)**: A super heavy-lift expendable launch vehicle developed to carry the Orion spacecraft into translunar injection orbit. It is the most powerful rocket launched by NASA since the Saturn V.
2. **Orion Spacecraft**: The crew capsule designed to support up to four astronauts for deep-space missions lasting up to 21 days untethered.
3. **Gateway Space Station**: A planned small space station in a Near-Rectilinear Halo Orbit (NRHO) around the Moon. Gateway will serve as a multi-purpose outpost supporting crew transfers, science experiments, and lunar landing staging.
4. **Human Landing System (HLS)**: NASA selected SpaceX's Starship HLS variant to carry the first Artemis astronauts from lunar orbit down to the surface of the Moon for the Artemis III mission.

## Mission Architecture
- **Artemis I**: An uncrewed test flight successfully conducted in late 2022. It tested the SLS Block 1 rocket and sent the Orion capsule on a 25-day journey around the Moon before splashing down safely in the Pacific Ocean.
- **Artemis II**: The first crewed mission scheduled to fly four astronauts around the Moon in a free-return trajectory without landing.
- **Artemis III**: The milestone mission planned to land the first woman and the first person of color on the lunar South Pole region, conducting surface scientific research and water ice prospecting.
